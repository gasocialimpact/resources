Three Points on the Impact Investing Lifecycle

GSIC's Foundation Impact Investing Starter Kit is a growing education and action resource hub that can bring value to foundations at three distinct points on the impact investing lifecycle:

  1. Raising Awareness and Building Internal Will
  2. Transforming Interest to Action
  3. Learning from Early Impact Investing Efforts and Evolving Towards Better Practice

Moving from Interest to a Deeper Visioning Process

Foundations often mistakenly assume they need to have all the answers before "interest in impact investing" can move into a deeper visioning process, resulting in a protracted, frustrating educational journey. It's unrealistic to expect that informal, unstructured education will answer every key question that defines the contours of an impact investing strategy. Clarifying what you want to change about your investments — whether it's ensuring that your public holdings are not fueling harmful industries and actors, or finding high-impact values-aligned fund managers, or bringing more capital back to Georgia, or all of the above — also clarifies what steps your foundation needs to take to bring about that change.

If Local or Place-Based Impact Investing is most important to your foundation, the question set below outlines the full scope of decisions that eventually shape an impact investing strategy. It is offered as a map to guide the foundation's education and strategy development process, not as a prescriptive checklist that works for every foundation. Some of the additional tools in the Foundation Impact Investing Starter Kit, like the Source of Funds Calculator and the Impact Investing Deal Bank, were designed to support productive, data-informed impact investing discussion.

The Questions That Shape an Impact Investing Strategy

Success Statements

  • What community impact outcomes are we seeking in our work?
  • What strategic business outcomes are we seeking in our work?
  • Which outcomes are most important?

Impact Focus & Capital Uses

  • What kind of investments will make a difference?
  • What sectors or topics are we trying to influence?
  • What communities (neighborhoods, towns, etc.) are the focus?
  • Who are we trying to reach and why?

Source of Funds

  • What size program are we trying to build? And how quickly?
  • What assets would we like to use?
  • What consequences do we anticipate from using that source?
  • Is it just investments, or do we need grant resources too?
  • When investments are repaid, where do the assets go?

Investment Criteria & Guidelines

  • What are our expectations around return, risk, and term?
  • Who are our likely community partners?
  • What steps can we take to ensure success?
  • How will we assess opportunities? What criteria matter?
  • What happens if an investment goes bad?

Governance & Policy

  • What process will we follow to make investment decisions?
  • Who will make investment decisions?
  • What reporting do the board, staff, and committees require?
  • What expertise is needed to oversee this program?
  • What policies need to be put in place?

Staffing & Resourcing

  • How will we cover the costs of local investing activities?
  • How will we source, assess, and service investments?
  • What programmatic staffing will we require?
  • What finance and accounting support will we need?
  • What external assistance might we need?
